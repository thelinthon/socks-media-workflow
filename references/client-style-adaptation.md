# Client Style Adaptation

Use this reference when the user asks to match prior client emails, reports, or team conventions.

## Retrieval boundary

Search only the authorized mailbox, folder, client, subject pattern, and date range. Prefer messages actually sent by the user or approved team examples. Do not infer a client's preference from inbound messages alone.

Do not store or package raw customer emails, recipient addresses, signatures, confidential notices, account IDs, links, or campaign results in a reusable skill. Extract a minimal style profile and keep source locators for audit.

## Style profile

Record only reusable attributes:

- greeting and closing convention;
- language and tone;
- typical length and detail level;
- information order;
- preferred headings and bullet structure;
- whether weekly and cumulative metrics are both shown;
- usual treatment of audience, creative, placement, pacing, issues, and next steps;
- phrases the user consistently removes, adds, or softens.

Require multiple examples before treating a pattern as stable. Label one-example patterns as provisional.

Classify the example before learning from it:

- **User-authored:** may inform the user's personal drafting style.
- **Team-authored:** may inform an approved team pattern, but not the user's personal voice.
- **Client-authored:** informs client questions, sensitivities, and requested follow-up—not the agency's writing style.
- **Forwarded thread:** preserve the author and date of each quoted message; do not treat the forwarder as the original author.

Client feedback in the thread overrides assumptions about what the client needs next. Convert unanswered questions or requested remedies into explicit tasks with owner, deadline, evidence, and approval need.

## Client-ready weekly email

When supported by the approved examples, use this compact structure:

1. Greeting and attachment/reference line.
2. Material issue or decision first, including confirmed response.
3. Campaign sections ordered by consequence or the client's established convention.
4. For each campaign: overall weekly result, cumulative result/pacing, then only material audience, creative, or placement findings.
5. Required decision, next action, or monitoring statement.
6. Established closing.

Preserve the user's edited draft as the current authority when it differs from an earlier generated draft. Create a draft only; do not send without explicit authorization and final recipient/content/attachment verification.
