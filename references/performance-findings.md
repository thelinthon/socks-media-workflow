# Performance Findings

Use this reference to turn verified media results into concise, evidence-based weekly findings.

## Analysis order

1. Overall delivery: weekly actuals, cumulative actuals, pacing, and KPI achievement.
2. Audience: material spend/volume contributors, efficient segments, and weak segments with sufficient volume.
3. Creative: compare formats or messages only on compatible objectives, periods, audiences, and placements.
4. Placement: distinguish scale contribution from efficiency; do not call a small-volume placement a winner without qualification.
5. Decision: monitor, investigate, prepare an adjustment, or close out; name owner and approval boundary.

## Claim structure

For every material point, retain:

- **Fact:** verified metric, period, comparison, and source.
- **Possible cause:** plausible explanation, evidence, confidence, and alternative explanation.
- **To confirm:** missing evidence required to establish the cause or action.
- **Recommendation:** proposed next step, expected effect, trade-off, owner, deadline, and approval.

Do not turn correlation into causation. A high-spend audience is a volume contributor, not automatically the best audience. A low cost with tiny delivery is a signal to test, not proof it will scale.

## Language guardrails

Prefer calibrated language such as `stable`, `above/below plan`, `more efficient within this comparison`, or `requires monitoring`.

Avoid unsupported superlatives such as `perfect`, `crushed`, `absolute winner`, `extremely strong`, or `amazing`. Use them only when an approved threshold and sufficient scale make the claim defensible.

If a known setup error affected performance, state the confirmed error, measured impact, containment, and recovery plan separately. Do not promise KPI recovery unless the responsible human has approved the commitment.

## Incident and remediation findings

When a weekly-report thread contains an execution incident, separate:

1. **Confirmed incident:** what should have happened, what actually happened, affected dates/campaigns, and evidence.
2. **Measured impact:** incremental spend or delivery, KPI effect, and what cannot yet be quantified.
3. **Containment:** confirmed action already completed, including timestamp when available.
4. **Root cause:** confirmed process/system cause or a labeled hypothesis with owner for investigation.
5. **Remedy:** options, trade-offs, approval owner, and client communication status.
6. **Open client question:** exact requested resolution and response deadline.

Do not treat `the metric improved after an audience or creative was paused` as proof the pause caused the improvement unless the comparison controls for period, spend, auction conditions, placements, and other simultaneous changes. Use `performance remained/improved after the change` and label causality as an inference.
