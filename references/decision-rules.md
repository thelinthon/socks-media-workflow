# Decision and Prioritization Rules

Apply team- or client-approved thresholds when supplied. Do not invent universal pacing, KPI, budget, or optimization thresholds. When none exists, quantify the observed variance, label threshold status as `Not defined`, and request a decision only if the ambiguity changes action.

## Priority

| Level | Use when | Default response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P0 | Active spend, legal/privacy, material financial error, or client-facing delivery is immediately at risk and delay compounds harm | Surface first; identify accountable owner and immediate containment; require human decision |
| P1 | A near-term deadline, blocked dependency, significant performance exception, or setup issue could materially affect delivery | Resolve or escalate within the current work window |
| P2 | Important planned work with a clear deadline but no immediate material risk | Schedule with owner and dependency |
| P3 | Useful improvement, documentation, exploration, or optimization without near-term consequence | Backlog or bundle with related work |

Never assign urgency from tone alone. Consider deadline, consequence, reversibility, dependency, spend/delivery exposure, and certainty.

## Decision tests by work type

### Weekly reporting and QC

- Confirm reporting window, platform freshness, currency, formula integrity, and plan/source totals before interpreting performance.
- Treat missing KPI, mismatched totals, duplicated rows, broken formulas, and incomplete platform data as explicit exceptions.
- If two sources disagree, do not average them. Prepare a reconciliation table and identify who chooses the authoritative source.

### MTD execution and pacing

- Calculate elapsed-time pacing only when the active dates, planned budget, actual spend, and timezone are known.
- Separate underspend/overspend caused by setup, delivery, data latency, paused periods, or plan changes.
- Recommend monitoring, investigation, or adjustment based on approved thresholds and remaining time. Do not recommend a live budget change from variance alone.

### Performance overview

- Compare against the approved KPI or relevant historical baseline, not whichever comparison looks most dramatic.
- Separate signal from measurement noise, attribution differences, and data incompleteness.
- Offer alternative explanations when evidence does not isolate a cause.

### Ad setup decisions

- Verify objective, market, dates, budget, audience, placements, optimization event, tracking, naming, assets, landing page, and approvals.
- Mark a setup `Not ready` when a missing item could create incorrect spend, measurement, targeting, or client representation.
- Prepare the setup recommendation and checklist; leave creation, activation, bid/budget changes, and publishing for authorized human execution.

### Demos and content optimization

- Tie each demo or content recommendation to an audience/job, observed evidence, intended change, measurement plan, and owner.
- Distinguish platform best practice, client convention, and the agent's hypothesis.
- Avoid presenting a creative preference as a performance conclusion without evidence.

## Recommendation format

Use this compact structure:

1. **Action:** what should happen.
2. **Evidence:** facts supporting it and source references.
3. **Expected effect:** what should improve or what risk is contained.
4. **Trade-off:** downside, cost, or opportunity cost.
5. **Confidence:** high / medium / low with reason.
6. **Owner and deadline:** who decides or executes, and by when.
7. **Approval:** explicit approval required before any external or live-system effect.
